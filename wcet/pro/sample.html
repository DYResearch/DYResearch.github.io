Method and assumptions

Response-time analysis over the level-i busy period (Lehoczky, 1990), with release jitter (Tindell, Burns and Wellings, 1994), in exact integer arithmetic. Engine: dy-wcet 4.1.6, open source; its answers are checked against an independent oracle, an exhaustive search of small task sets and a simulated scheduler, and eight bounded Kani proofs gate every change to it.

  • Fixed-priority preemptive scheduling on one processor core. Priority is the order of the tasks, highest first.
  • Execution times, jitter and blocking are inputs. DY-WCET Pro does not measure them; the verdict is as good as the numbers it is given.
  • Kernel overheads — context switches, interrupt entry, tick handling — are included in the execution times or blocking given.
  • Blocking is the longest time a task can wait for a lower-priority task holding a shared resource, as under the priority ceiling protocol.
  • Deadlines may be shorter or longer than periods. A response that runs past its period is analysed over the whole busy period.
  • Values that were not whole microseconds were rounded toward the safe side: execution, jitter and blocking up; periods and deadlines down.
